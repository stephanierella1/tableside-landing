01Plan the week

The week, decided.

Sunday’s twenty-minute debate becomes one tap. Tableside fills the nights you’re cooking from recipes you already love.

i.

Pick your cooking days.

Cooking six nights this week? Three? Tell it which nights, and leave the rest for leftovers, takeout or dinner out.

ii.

Shuffle the week, or one night.

Fill every night in one tap. Not feeling Wednesday? Shuffle just that one and keep the rest.

iii.

No repeats.

It steers clear of what you just had, so Tuesday doesn’t taste like Monday.

02Your recipes

Every recipe, one shelf.

Everything you cook in one place, not 47 browser tabs. Bring recipes in however they live in your life.

  • Paste a link.From any recipe site. The ingredients and steps come in for you.
  • Describe it from memory.Grandma’s chili, that dish from the place downtown. Tell it what you remember and it shapes it into a recipe.
  • Or write it by hand.A blank page, every field yours.
  • Photos, ratings and notes.Everyone at the table rates what they ate, and your notes (“double the sauce”) stay with the recipe.
03The grocery list

The list writes itself.

Plan the week and your grocery list is already done, built from every dinner on the menu.

  • Sorted by aisle.Produce with produce, dairy with dairy. Check things off as you go.
  • Skips your pantry staples.Tell it what you always keep on hand and it stops adding olive oil to the list.
  • One list for the house.Whoever makes it to the store has the same list.
04Cook Mode

One step at a time, hands full.

When it’s time to cook, the recipe steps aside for Cook Mode: large type on a dark walnut screen, one step at a time.

  • Built-in timers.Tap an underlined time in a step and the timer starts.
  • Just this step’s ingredients.Measured out right below the step, so you’re not scrolling back to the top with garlic on your hands.
  • Your notes come along.The reminder you left yourself last time is right at the top.
05Your table

Who sits at your table.

Tableside is built for a household, not one person with a recipe box. Bring everyone in: up to six people, from the ones who cook to the ones who just eat.

  • Everyone’s preferences.Each person at the table has their own, from the partner who rates everything to the toddler who eats only noodles.
  • One plan, one list.Shared in real time, so nobody plans dinner twice and nobody buys milk twice.
